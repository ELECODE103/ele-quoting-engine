STRAIGHTEDGE — round 2: three gesture-led directions

Each mark is one shape that can stand alone without letters. Shown in the recommended palette (Ink & Brass), one-color, on dark, at 20 mm, as a favicon, on a shelf beside neutral competitors, on a packing slip, embroidered, and as a 6 mm part stamp. The last section shows direction E in all three palettes so color can be judged apart from form.

Palettes

1 · Ink & Brass — recommendedDeep ink navy with a warm brass accent on bone paper. The colors of an instrument case: the gauge, the dial, the nameplate. Reads expensive and settled, and no shop in the directory owns it.#101C33 · #A8834E · #F4F1EA
2 · Charcoal & Layout BlueCharcoal with the round-1 Dykem layout-fluid blue as the single signal. Cooler and more technical — reads "engineering" before "heritage". Safe, credible, slightly common.#25282D · #1F418F · #FFFFFF
3 · Machine Green & BoneThe gray-green of old iron (Bridgeport, South Bend) on bone — the color of natural acetal stock. Quiet and very credible to a machinist; the least "brand-like" of the three.#15171A · #2F4A3C · #EDE7D6

D · Knife Edge

The straightedge as one stroke the name rests on — true edge dead level on top, heel cut square, far end ground to a knife point. Oswald SemiBold.

E · The Kerf

A square of stock parted by one dead-straight cut; nothing moved, the halves still make a square. Archivo Bold, set tight.

F · On the Plate

The straightedge standing dead square on the surface plate (an engineer reads ⟂). The name is custom-drawn: straight strokes only, 45° machined chamfers where a curve would be.
