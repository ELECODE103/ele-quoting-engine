# LinkedIn post 4: what the router is for (day 17)

## Paste this

A lot of flat plastic parts get made the expensive way.

If your part is flat, an inch thick or less, and has pockets or counterbores, it belongs on a router. Milling it from a block means paying to turn most of the material into chips. A laser or a waterjet cuts all the way through; it can't cut a pocket partway.

A router does the outline, the pockets and the counterbores in one pass over the sheet, and the price drops when the quantity fills a whole sheet.

You can check this yourself. Upload the part at straightedgemfg.com and switch the process between machining and routing. The price changes on the screen. If routing is cheaper, that's the one to buy.

The router bed is 59" × 96". The quote tool prices sheet from 1/16" to 1" thick.
