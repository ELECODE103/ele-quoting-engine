An engineer on Eng-Tips once asked whether the flatness he wanted on a plastic plate was "asking too much." The honest answer is that it depends.

Here is what it depends on in my shop.

Acetal (Delrin): [CONFIRM: tol-acetal]. It's the plastic to pick when a dimension matters.

Nylon: [CONFIRM: tol-nylon]. It grows when it takes on moisture, so tell me if it runs wet.

UHMW and HDPE: [CONFIRM: tol-uhmw] [CONFIRM: tol-hdpe]. On anything long, tell me the temperature it runs at. They move with the weather.

PTFE: [CONFIRM: tol-ptfe]. It creeps under its own clamping.

PEEK: [CONFIRM: tol-peek]. Holds size well, and the stock costs enough that I'd rather talk before I cut.

Two things lower the price of almost any plastic part. Put the tight number only on the feature that needs it. And tell me what the part does, because the right material often fixes what a tighter tolerance can't.

If a print asks for something the material won't keep, I'll say so before I cut it.
