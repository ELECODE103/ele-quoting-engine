## Owner decisions, 2026-11-11 (these apply to every piece)

1. **Nothing about certification or inspection equipment the shop lacks.** No line about being or not being certified to any standard, in either direction, and no line about equipment that is not there. Say what is done, and say it as an option: "A first-article inspection report and a signed certificate of conformance come with your order when you ask for them." Never "with every order": the customer ticks a box on the quote or on the firm-quote form, and it adds a fee the quote shows before they pay (owner, 2026-11-11, on reviewing the draft). Do not print the dollar amount; it is set in the pricing console. "Material traceable to the lot." stands.
2. **Nothing about the size of the shop.** No headcount in either direction, and no invented staff either ("our team", "our engineers", "our quality department"). The shop is "we". Direct access is said as engineer-led: "Your quote and your parts are handled by the engineer who answers the phone." In the two one-to-one emails you sign with your own name, "I" is you writing the note; the shop is still "we".
3. **Turning is not sold as its own operation**: "milled and routed". Anodize and powder coat are sent out; bead blasting is done here. Standard lead time 7 to 10 business days, expedited 3 to 5. A person quotes within 24 hours when the instant price does not apply.
4. **Replacement parts are quoted from the physical part.** "Send us the part", never "send a photo". The street address is given after a request is sent (on the confirmation screen and in the email); do not post it. The customer pays to ship the part in; we send the original back with the finished parts, and the price we quote includes that return shipping.
